# Phase 0 Pre-Screen: The 9-Dimension Serial-Founder Gate

A fast, cheap gut-check that runs BEFORE the expensive 7-persona teardown. Score each
dimension with a quick verdict, then render a three-way Build / Sleep-on-it / Pass gate. Only
Build (or a user override) earns the deep dive.

The gate is driven by the **4 CORE dimensions** (Problem, Audience, Monetization, Moat). The
**5 strategic lenses** (Wedge, Distribution, Portfolio-fit, Energy-fit, Opportunity-cost) are
**ADVISORY** in Phase 0 - they sharpen the rationale and flag the biggest unknown, but none of
them hard-vetoes the gate. Energy-fit in particular is NOT a Phase-0 veto; it becomes a
documented modifier in the Step 8 final verdict, along with the other four lenses.

> Pre-screen adapted from Corey Haines / makerskills `business-brainstorm`.

## The discipline: name it or mark it unknown

The value of this pre-screen is honesty about what is NOT yet known. For each dimension,
you must either **name a concrete, specific answer** or mark it **UNKNOWN**. Do NOT pad a
dimension with a plausible-sounding assumption to make the idea look more ready than it is.
A wall of confident guesses is exactly the failure this gate exists to catch.

- If you can name the specific customer, channel, wedge, or number: state it.
- If you cannot: mark UNKNOWN and treat it as a real gap, not a rounding error.
- Two or more UNKNOWN dimensions is itself a signal - the idea is under-formed, and the
  right move is usually Sleep-on-it (go gather the missing facts) rather than Pass.

## Verdict symbols (per dimension)

| Symbol  | Meaning |
|---------|---------|
| PASS    | Clear, credible, specific answer. Strength. |
| CAUTION | Answer exists but is soft, unproven, or carries a real risk. |
| FAIL    | Answer is missing where it must exist, or is actively negative. |
| UNKNOWN | Not yet known. An honest gap, not a guess. |

This is the SAME four-state vocabulary used in the Step 8 final scorecard - there is no
separate "WATCH" state. (If you have seen WATCH in an older run, read it as CAUTION.) The four
states map cleanly to the check / soft / cross / question conventions.

## The 9 dimensions

1. **Problem** - Is the pain real, acute, and frequent, and is the affected population
   large enough to matter? A vitamin (nice-to-have) fails here; a hair-on-fire problem
   passes. Name who feels it and how often.

2. **Audience** - Can you name the buyer specifically, reach them, and do they hold
   budget? "SMBs" is UNKNOWN; "solo tattoo artists booked 2-3 weeks out in urban US
   markets" is a PASS-shaped answer. Reachable AND has budget - both required.

3. **Wedge** - What is the minimum viable offer that wedges into the customer's existing
   workflow? Not the full vision - the smallest thing they would adopt next week without
   ripping out what they already use. If you can only describe the grand platform, mark
   the wedge UNKNOWN.

4. **Monetization** - How does value get captured, and do the unit economics work? Name
   the model, a price point, and whether the margin survives delivery cost. "We'll figure
   out pricing later" is UNKNOWN and a CAUTION at best.

5. **Moat** - What stops a well-resourced copycat six months after you prove the market?
   Data, network effects, switching cost, distribution lock, brand, regulatory. "First
   mover" is not a moat - mark it FAIL or UNKNOWN unless something compounds.

6. **Portfolio fit** - Does this COMPOUND with what the founder already runs (shared
   audience, tech, distribution, brand), or does it FRAGMENT focus into a new unrelated
   surface? Ground this in the founder's real properties (see `portfolio.local.md`). For
   an operator with existing products, a fragmenting idea is a CAUTION even when strong on
   its own merits.

7. **Distribution** - How do the FIRST 100 customers actually hear about this? Name the
   specific channel and why it works for THIS audience (not "content marketing" in the
   abstract). If the answer is "we'll do ads and hope," mark it UNKNOWN.

8. **Energy fit** - Is the founder genuinely energized to work on this for years, or
   chasing a shiny object? Honest self-assessment. A brilliant idea the founder will
   abandon in month 4 is a FAIL on the only dimension that survives every pivot.

9. **Opportunity cost** - What high-leverage work does building this DISPLACE? Every yes
   is a no to something else. Name what gets starved (the existing product, the day job,
   the higher-EV bet) so the trade is explicit, not accidental.

## Rendering the gate verdict

Tally the dimensions, then apply judgment (this is a gate, not arithmetic). The verdict is
driven by the **4 CORE dimensions (Problem, Audience, Monetization, Moat) only**. The 5
strategic lenses are advisory here: they inform the rationale and the biggest-unknown call,
but they do NOT hard-veto the gate (Energy-fit included).

- **BUILD** - all 4 core dimensions are PASS, with Problem and Audience both PASS, and no more
  than ~1 UNKNOWN or CAUTION among the core four. Proceed to the full 7-persona teardown.
- **SLEEP ON IT** - The shape is promising but 2+ core dimensions are UNKNOWN or CAUTION. The
  right next move is to go name the unknowns, not to burn the deep-dive on an under-formed
  idea. STOP and offer the user an explicit yes/no choice to override and run the teardown
  anyway.
- **PASS** - Problem or Audience is FAIL, or 3+ core dimensions are UNKNOWN/FAIL. The idea is
  not ready for (or worth) the expensive teardown as scoped. Say so plainly and, if useful,
  point at the single dimension that would have to change.

A dominant strength on one lens does not rescue a FAIL on Problem or Audience - those two
core dimensions are the load-bearing gate. A weak lens (e.g. a soft Energy-fit or a
fragmenting Portfolio-fit) is recorded and carried into Step 8 as a modifier, but it does not
by itself block a BUILD.

## Why these 5 were the gap

The original 7-persona teardown already probes Problem (Prompts 1-2), Audience (Prompt 2),
Monetization (Prompt 3), Moat (Prompt 4), Team (Prompt 5), Survival (Prompt 6), and Pitch
(Prompt 7). The pre-screen adds five lenses the teardown did not cover:

- **Wedge** - the minimum viable entry point, distinct from the full pricing model.
- **Distribution** - the first-100-customers channel, distinct from survival-scale CAC.
- **Portfolio fit** - compound vs fragment, critical for a multi-product operator.
- **Energy fit** - founder durability, the axis that outlives every pivot.
- **Opportunity cost** - what building this starves.

These five also feed the Step 8 final scorecard so nothing surfaced in Phase 0 is lost.
One caveat: the Phase-0 **Distribution** score is a quick pre-screen only. In Step 8 it is
SUPERSEDED by Prompt 6's go-to-market evidence (customer count, ARPU, CAC, channel), which is
scored in the "Survival Path" row - so Distribution is NOT re-added as an independent Step 8
modifier (that would double-count go-to-market). Its Phase-0 verdict is still preserved
verbatim in the Step 8 Phase-0 block for continuity.
